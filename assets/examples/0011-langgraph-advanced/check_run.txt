answer: **Recommendation: Start with a plain tool loop, and migrate to LangGraph only when a concrete operational requirement appears—most likely checkpointing or a human approval gate.**

**Reasons:**
1. **The loop is cheaper to build and read.** Multiple sources agree a `while` loop over `bind_tools` covers most single-turn support flows, and setup time favors the loop. Every graph node is another place to test.
2. **The graph's payoff is operational, not benchmark-driven.** Practitioners say a graph "pays off at the second operational requirement, usually resume or approval." Checkpointers (MemorySaver for demos, PostgresSaver for production) let a run pause before a side effect and resume from another worker—valuable once a deploy kills a long job or an approval waits days.
3. **Migration is low-risk.** Teams reportedly move the loop into a graph without changing prompts, so starting simple doesn't lock you in.

**Caveat:** No source quantifies build or maintenance effort, latency, or a team-size threshold, so the trigger for migrating is judgment, not data. Write down your state fields and reducers before coding—that's the hidden cost.

**Missing/empty sources:** None of the three result sets was empty, but all lack cost, latency, and team-size comparisons, and Reddit offers no account of a failed migration.
